---
title: Philosophy
description: What Treebank measures, and what it publishes.
order: 10
---

**Measure all three directions.** Does the grammar accept valid code, does it
reject what the language rejects, and does it build the right tree? They fail
independently, so they are measured separately.

**The reference is the language's own toolchain.** CPython, `javac`, `rustc`,
`tsc`. A disagreement between two independent parsers tells you nothing about
which one is right.

**Evidence is committed, not reported.** A ledger bound to a grammar revision,
over a corpus pinned by a lockfile, can be reproduced. It also goes visibly
stale when the grammar moves.

**Generated things are generated.** Sweep results are regenerated by running
the sweep. The CI matrix is read off the checkout. The grammar reference is
rendered from the parse table. Every hand-maintained copy is somewhere the
repository can start being wrong quietly.

**Lists are discovered.** A directory under `crates/` with a `grammar.js` in
it is a grammar — for the gates, the wasm packs, and the pages on this site. A
hand-kept list means a new grammar's checks pass because nothing ran them.

**Failures are written down.** Where a grammar cannot handle something — an
unexpanded macro, a toolchain with no tree dump — it is declared in a policy
file and shown on that grammar's page. An undeclared deviation fails the
build.
